What makes a password strong

Unpredictability beats cleverness

A password is strong when there are so many possibilities that guessing would take forever. Swapping letters for symbols in a real word doesn't help much, because attackers try those tricks first. Pure randomness does.

Passwords or passphrases?

A random password packs a lot of strength into a short string, which suits a password manager. A passphrase strings together random words like staple-ocean-bravery-lantern-quartz-hammock. It's long but easy to type and remember, and six random words are already very hard to guess.

Where the quantum part comes in

Computers usually make random numbers from a formula. This lab is connecting a real quantum random number generator, so each character can come from a quantum measurement. When it's online, the quantum numbers are mixed with your browser's own secure randomness, so the result is never weaker than either one, and nobody along the way can work out your password.

The strength meter

The estimate assumes an attacker who can try 100 billion guesses a second, about what a serious cracking rig can manage against a weak password store.
